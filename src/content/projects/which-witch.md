## 01 — Project Context

A Unity potion-shop project with the UCI Video Game Design Club, developed over approximately one academic quarter by a team of more than ten students. I was the project's only Game Designer.

When I joined, the team had work underway but lacked a clear gameplay direction. I first assessed its existing state and needs, then defined a coherent player experience for the team to build toward.

## 02 — Design Problem

Running a shop needed a reason to matter: a clear player goal, decisions about ingredients and orders, and an economy that connected everyday actions with progression.

## 03 — My Decision: A Three-Day Rivalry

I defined the objective: **earn more money than the neighboring witch after three in-game days.** A rival and a deadline gave the management design a concrete motivation, pace, and win condition.

**Buy ingredients → brew potions → sell potions → earn money → purchase upgrades → repeat.**

## 04 — Economy & Player Decisions

I designed ingredient purchasing, potion crafting, order fulfillment, upgrades, and their economic relationships. The intended choices were which ingredients to buy, which potions to make, which orders to prioritize, and when to spend income on upgrades.

The three-day structure gave these decisions a shared time horizon. I used it to frame pacing while addressing limited decision space, repetitive actions, and economic balance as design problems.

## 05 — Design-to-Implementation

I defined the gameplay rules and overall experience; other team members implemented the features. My contribution was connecting the loop, economy, win condition, and pacing into a direction the team could work from.

<figure><img src="../../images/which-witch/brewing.webp" alt="Brewing interface with a liquid inventory, input slots, progress bars, and Brew button." loading="lazy" width="917" height="569" /><figcaption>The brewing interface connects available ingredients with potion production.</figcaption></figure>

## 06 — Delivered Work

The team completed some systems and a playable foundation, available on itch.io. The broader design remained only partly implemented when graduating team members reduced the project's development capacity.

## 07 — Design Takeaway

This project shows how I work within an existing production: identify missing design foundations, understand available resources, and define goals and system rules around a coherent player experience.
